# Boundless Blue — notes for developers and LLMs

(Renamed from "Endless Ocean" on 2026-11-01 to avoid clashing with an existing game series; repo `ethanpil/boundless-blue`.)

Static site, no build step. The three.js version is pinned in the `index.html` import map (0.186.1). Deployed with GitHub Pages from `main` (root).

## Invariants (breaking these causes subtle bugs)
- **Coordinates:** travel distance `s` grows forward, and global `z = -s`. Everything that lives in the world is a child of the `world` group, at global coordinates.
- **Floating origin:**
  - `world.position.z = -Oz`, where `Oz` is a multiple of **1024**.
  - Shader-space patterns (caustics, noise texture, ripples) must use periods that divide 1024. Otherwise they jump at each rebase.
  - Never feed absolute global positions to shaders; use `vWPos`, which is render-space.
- **Shader time** `U.uTime` wraps every 4096 s, for float precision. Visible pops once an hour are accepted.
- **Colours:** biome colours are authored in sRGB and converted to linear in `biomes.js`. Vertex colours and uniforms are linear. Forgetting this washes out the image badly; this happened early on.
- **Materials:** every lit material goes through `applyWater(mat, { key })`, and `key` must be unique per shader variant. three caches programs by `customProgramCacheKey`, and a shared key silently reuses the wrong shader.
- **Flight tube rules:**
  - Solid tall objects keep ≥ 3.5 m from the path line (`pathX(s)`, `pathY(s)`).
  - Low props may sit under the path if their top stays ≥ 1.3 m below `pathY`.
  - Swaying vegetation may fill the tube; only a ~0.6 m core around the camera line stays free.
  - Tall "go-around" obstacles sit on the outside of bends (see `pathCurv`), so they appear dead ahead and the camera curves past them.
  - `floorAt` and `ceilingAt` clamp near the path.
- **Chunks:**
  - Chunks are 64 m long and built by generators under a per-frame time budget.
  - Populators must stay at or under about 5 ms.
  - A chunk can be unloaded while its build is still queued; `terrain.update` drops such builds.

## Why things are the way they are
- **Sky-visibility map** (128² texels at 2 m, CPU-computed, camera-local): lets every material and the god rays know whether a cave or ice ceiling is overhead. This costs much less than shadows.
- **ROV lamps** (`uLamp`): the deep zones and caves are physically too dark to watch, so lamps mounted beside the camera light the scene, as in documentary footage.
  - Auto mode (`updateEnvironment` in main.js) also estimates the light reaching the scene (sun and sky through the water above the camera) and switches the lamps on when it is low. The threshold rises with turbidity (`uSigma.y`), so murky mangrove, kelp or bloom water gets lamps at dusk while clear reef water waits for night (`C.lampAdd`).
  - In turbid zones the in-scatter colours (`deep`, `up`) carry most of the frame, so a dark palette there reads as a black screen. Mangrove was 0x2b4a35 and gave a mean luminance of ~40 at 2 m by day.
- **Caustics texture channels:** R is the fine, fast network (shallows), G the coarse network (deep), B the blurred focusing bands (the shaft mask). `causticRGB` blends R into G with depth and flattens contrast with `uCloud`. Low takes one tap, with no dispersion.
- **Shafts** sample B with `textureLod`: derivatives are meaningless inside the jittered march and always picked the 1x1 mip, which made the shafts a flat glow. They march the first 40 m and add the rest as an analytic tail. Beam contrast follows `uCloud` and sun elevation.
- **Swell focusing** (`SWELL_GLSL` / `swellFocus` in water.js, part of `WATER_PARS`): the direct sun brightens and dims by up to 15% near the surface with two slow swells whose wave vectors divide 1024; it fades by 22 m and under cloud. The seabed and the sun-lit marine snow use the same term: change it in one place. `cloudShade` beside it adds moving cloud shadows on the shallows under broken cloud only (`uCloud` 0.35-0.8; up to 70% of the direct sun, which at 8 m makes the shaded stretch 6-7% darker and its caustics weaker; gone by 45 m), shared with the sun-lit snow and the god rays.
- **Surge that everything shares:** one 11 s back-and-forth beat. `pSurge` in props/materials.js moves every `MAT.sway` style except tube worms (a travelling 57 m swell along x, the kelp streaming axis, with a gust envelope; weaker with depth, scaled by `uSurge`; per-plant flutter on top), and the marine snow and near motes in env.js sway on the same beat. Wave vectors are whole multiples of 2π/1024. It is exported as `SURGE` and also used by the sargassum and anemone shaders in nb3_common.js: a new sway shader should include it rather than add its own surge term.
- **Lamp beams in three places:** the lamp haze (post.js shafts pass), the lamp lighting of snow and motes (`lampBeams` in env.js) and `LIGHT_PATCH` (water.js) each carry the two side-lamp positions and cones: move the lamps in all three together.
  - On low the shafts pass marches the lamp haze at 2 beams x 4 samples (8 cone evaluations, the cost before the two-beam shape); medium and up use 2 x 8.
- **Small things in the water** (env.js): on medium and up the snow samples the caustics B channel in its vertex shader, so flakes brighten inside sun shafts and dim (to 0.35x at most) between them. Near motes with `aRand.w > 0.85` are small rising bubbles in a 4 m box just ahead of the lens (same draw call), more as the craft speeds up, hidden under a roof via the sky-visibility map. The rim of Snell's window takes the rain-ring slope and, with surge, the fine chop. The composite's contrast rises by up to 7% under a low sun. Snow alpha follows a 32-34 m patch field (denser and thinner water drifting past), stronger in turbid water. Outside Snell's window the surface mirrors the seabed colour in the shallows (tan patches when looking up in kelp are that mirror, not the canopy).
- **Near motes:** marine snow includes `count/60` soft bokeh motes in an 8 m box; the box must divide 1024.
- **Vignette:** the old one dimmed the outer third of the frame to 30%, which crushed murky zones to black at the edges. Zones were tuned around it, so frames are now a little brighter overall.
- **Context loss:** props free their CPU vertex arrays after upload, so a lost WebGL context cannot be refilled. main.js pauses while the context is lost and, once restored, reloads with the same seed and the current zone.
- **URL params** go through `num()` (non-finite values fall back to the default); unknown `quality` or `biome` values are ignored.
- **Per-segment set pieces** that should start in view of the camera use `seg.camStart`, never `seg.id === 0`: ids are never reused, so after a zone jump the first segment has a later id.
- **Prop slices:** every merge pipeline (`staged`, `stagedNB`, `stagedNB2`) Z-sorts batch items with `spatialSort()` after registration, so slices are compact and frustum culling can drop them. On low, `finish` / `finishNB` put near/mid slices in a `THREE.LOD` (45/100 m), and slices whose largest item is under 3.5 m (`userData.itemR`) stop at 150 m. Big items and set pieces keep the fog distance; obstacles and labels are never culled.
- **Creature LOD (low / medium):** `_lodTick` picks levels by apparent size. Creatures are about twice as expensive per triangle as props.
  - Low: level 0 above 1/10 rad (1/6 for detail-2 species, at most 48 m), LOD -2 below 1/24 rad, and a speck mesh (LOD -3, fish-like plans only, about 100 triangles, no eyes) below 1/48 rad. Medium: only the speck, below 1/80 rad. High and ultra are unchanged.
  - Low, groups of 3+: the level comes from the nearest member in view (`lodD`), not from the bounding sphere, and members that want level 0 are drawn by a level-0 twin (up to 32). Before this, a school around the camera drew 300 sardines or 40 hammerheads at level 0.
  - Low `maxTris` is 170k (it was 260k); the headroom for new groups is about the same, because each animal costs about half.
- **Creature allocations:** the big per-agent methods (`_orient`, `_clear`, `_obsTop`, `_slotTarget`, `_zoneY`, `_columnClear`) are thin wrappers that pass doubles through the scratch array `_OV`, so the call boxes none of them; keep the wrappers tiny so callers inline them, and read `_OV` into locals at the top of each body. `Obstacles.add` creates `_cells`, `_q` and `_bb` up front: never add fields to obstacles later (three hidden classes caused "wrong map" deopts).
- **Adaptive resolution:** a step down within 12 s of a step up reverts exactly that step (a regular step overshot and climbed back). `dprChanged()` follows devicePixelRatio changes (matchMedia).
- **Low-tier kelp** thins only plants more than 10 m from the path: thinned beside it too, the camera crossed 20-30 m of bare sand between groves.
- **GPU tiers:** integrated and mobile GPUs (regex in `main.js`) get no MSAA, quarter-res shafts with 16 steps, and DPR ≤ 1. Measured on an Intel HD 4600 at 1024×576: about 7.5 ms per frame for the bare scene (no props or creatures). The full scene (2026-10-18) costs 10–15 ms depending on biome: pelagic ~10, reef ~15. Of that, the post pipeline is a fixed ~5 ms and visible triangles are ~0.25–0.6M.
- **Shadows (high tier only):**
  - A 2048² PCF sun shadow covers ±48 m around a point 32 m ahead of the camera, snapped to shadow texels to prevent shimmer.
  - Terrain marks opaque prop meshes as casting and receiving shadows. The floor only receives.
  - Shadow depth ignores vertex sway, so swaying plants cast static shadows. This is an accepted trade-off.
- **Distance softening:** the composite pass mixes in a quarter-res blurred scene by view depth (turbidity).
- **Finite colours:** phone GPUs produced NaN/Inf scene pixels (seen in Jellyfish Lake on Android), which showed as black squares with a bloom halo. `finiteCol()` in water.js (inside `waterFog` and the additive branch), the bloom bright pass and the composite's scene read all drop non-finite values (tests use comparisons, which are false for NaN) and clamp to 256, far inside half-float range. The source shader was not identified: it does not reproduce on desktop GPUs.
- **Load guard** (inline script in index.html): GitHub Pages lets each file be cached for 10 minutes (`max-age=600`), so right after a deploy a browser can link a new module against an old cached one; the import fails and main.js never runs (the title card sat at 0% forever). If `window.__ocean.ready` is missing 1.5 s after `load`, the guard re-fetches every loaded script with `cache: 'reload'` and reloads once (sessionStorage flag), then shows the error on the title card.
- **Dev server:** `python -m http.server` let the browser serve stale ES modules, so use a server that sends `Cache-Control: no-store`.

- **World workers** (`src/worker/`): 1-3 module workers (`WorkerPool`, hardwareConcurrency - 2) build the chunk heightfields and vertex data, the sky map and the noise texture; the main thread wraps the typed arrays in geometry and runs the populators.
  - Jobs carry `seq.snapshot()`. Before posting, the main thread calls `seq.weights` for every row the job reads, so its own sequence grows and prunes exactly as a local build would; the worker reads a `BiomeSequence.replica`, which never grows.
  - The worker's module graph (biomes.js, noise.js, `terrain_gen.js`) must not import `'three'`: import maps do not reach workers.
  - The same generators run on the main thread with `?worker=0`, without Worker (Node tools), after a worker error, in `prime()` (sync), and once the pool has not answered for 2 s (a synchronous loop such as `__ocean.advance` cannot receive worker messages).
- **Exact terrain sampling:** `shape.floorAt` / `ceilingAt` sample the rendered chunk meshes (same triangle split as the index buffer). This is O(1), allocation-free and exact, so anything placed with it cannot float. `floorExact` / `ceilingExact` are the analytic fallbacks, used before a chunk is built.
- **Per-segment variety:** `seq.weights(s)` entries carry the segment `g`. Biome `floor` / `ceil` receive `(x, s, ad, g, dx)`, and `sv(g, k)` gives deterministic per-segment randoms for shape, water clarity and tint.
- **LOD:** prop meshes tagged `userData.lod = 'near' | 'mid'` are hidden beyond per-tier distances (`LOD` in terrain.js). Untagged meshes are only fog-culled.
- **POIs:** `ctx.addPOI(type, x, y, s, r, data)` registers set pieces that creatures and the camera director use (`terrain.queryPOIs`).
- **Conditions** (`conditions.js`): a day/night cycle and weather produce multipliers that main.js applies. `U.uNight` and `U.uRain` are shared with props and creatures.
- **Camera = craft + head + look** (`camera.js`). Subjects come from `creatures.subjects(out)` and POIs; cooldowns stop it staring at one thing.
  - The craft's heading follows the route's direction averaged over +-32 m, centred `v x 2.8 s` ahead (`_routeDir`), through a jerk-limited `Follow` (9 deg/s, 3 deg/s^2): it eases into and out of bends and ignores the route's 38 m wiggles. It banks into turns (`15 deg x tanh(3.2 v w / 15 deg)`), slows up to 18% into tight bends and drifts at most `LAT_MAX` = 0.4 m off the route line.
  - The documentary gaze (subjects, POIs, moments, the whale-fall hold, the blue-hole gaze) is a head turn inside the craft (10 deg/s, 5 deg/s^2); craft plus head are capped at 13 deg/s and 8 deg/s^2. The viewer said the turns felt like a camera pan: the route itself turns gently (1-3 deg/s), and the pan was the old gaze spring, which stepped the angular acceleration on every subject switch (spikes of 170-190 deg/s^2) with a bank under 5 degrees that leaned out of the turn.
  - The viewer's look (drag to look, `app.lookMode` on / hold / off) is an offset from the craft heading (+-150 / +-70 degrees), 1:1 with the pointer, with a short coast on release; after 4 s idle it eases back unless held, and a double tap recentres. While it is active `look.w` suspends the director's gaze, including moments and the whale-fall hold: the viewer wins. `lookTick` runs on real frame time, so it works while paused and under `__ocean.advance`.
  - `director.heading` is the craft's forward vector (compass, sonar); `camState.forward` is the view direction, which can now point sideways or back. Anything that means "the way we are travelling" must use the heading.
  - The price is framing: the slower head lets a fast subject sit further off centre (p95 35-44 degrees, 58 in kelp, against 25-35 before).
  - Species fatigue (`camera.fatigue`, about 2.5 min time constant) stops one species from holding the camera: before it, sweepers took 58% of watch time in the cave and golden jellies 86% in Jellyfish Lake. Animals mid-event (play, strike, lunge, flip, roll) are offered at interest >= 0.9 and followed for up to 24 s; the camera used to watch only 23% of hunts.
- **Frame cap:** 60 fps by default (`?fps=`). The adaptive-resolution thresholds are relative to the cap. The frame delta comes from `THREE.Timer` (`THREE.Clock` is deprecated since r183 and warns on every load).
- **Floor detail:** ripple phases (`fRip` in terrain.js) are computed in the colour stage, where they darken troughs and lighten crests, and reused by the normal stage; fine ripples and sand-grain glints (4 cm hashed cells within 6 m, ids wrapped at 1024 m) are medium and up. `terrain_gen` adds shell-hash and diatom-film colour patches on sand (vertex colour). On sand it also tints by local concavity (paler silt in hollows, darker rises); the floor shader adds faint vertical seep streaks on steep rock.
- **Heightfield walls:** terrain is a 1.5 m grid, so a near-vertical step becomes straight grid-aligned edges over stretched triangles. Cenote skylights (`cave.ceil`) therefore place the rim by the approximate distance to the noise contour, `(o - th) / |grad o|`, and climb at ~65°.
- **Seamount pinnacles:** besides the 70 m lattice in `peaks()`, every seamount segment has path-side spires (`routePeaksOf`: one every 60-80 m, 15-34 m off the path on the outside of the sharpest bend, the first 45 m ahead of the camera via `segs[0].camStart`). `seamountPeaks(g, s0, s1)` lists both kinds. `nb2_seamount.js` finds summits on the live floor (`ctx.floorAt`), so every pinnacle the path passes gets its `pinnacle` POI and dressing.
- **Blue hole camera:** a `bluehole` POI counts as near as its rim when the director picks subjects. While it is watched, the gaze moves to a point ~30 m below the rim (pitch about -25° to -35° across the rim), then eases back to level toward the middle.
- **Share link** (`app.shareUrl`): origin + path with only `seed`, `biome` (and `start`). Quality, wake, fps and the time/weather locks are the viewer's own settings.
- **Wake lock:** a `NotAllowedError` on a request made from a user gesture is stored (`app.wakeLockError`) and requests stop until the option is toggled; a refusal on load is retried on the next tap or key. `app.wakeLock` is the *effective* setting (`wanted && !error`), so a blocked lock shows as off in the deck instead of "waiting" forever, and the toggle's tap clears the error and retries.
- **Staged boot:** `boot()` in main.js registers props, creates creatures/surprises/ambience and primes terrain with `await paint()` yields, so the title card's progress bar moves. `window.__ocean.ready` is that promise. `creatures`, `surprises` and `ambience` are `null` until it resolves, so anything that touches them must null-check.
- **Zone jump in place** (`app.jumpTo`): `terrain.reset()`, `creatures.reset()`, `surprises.reset()`, `director.reset()`, then `seq.restart(key, s)` and `terrain.primeAsync`. Segment ids come from `seq.nextId` and are never reused: ids used to be `segs.length`, so after pruning a new segment could inherit a dead one's id and its per-segment caches.
  - Nothing may look up a position from before the jump: the creatures' idle prewarm once read its stale `ctx.s` (kilometres ahead of the new start), which grew the restarted sequence and pruned the camera's own segment. The zone then had no props, the sky map went inactive with a job pending and `primeAsync` waited for it forever (`jumping` stuck). Prewarm now waits for the first real frame, and `SkyMap.update` drops its job when inactive.
- **Shader warm-up** (`precompile` in main.js): compile with `post.sceneRT` bound. The scene pass draws into a linear render target, which selects other program variants (output colour space) than no target; the old boot warm-up compiled variants nothing drew. Each chunk is precompiled when built (`terrain.onBuilt`); boot and zone jumps wait until programs are ready. Otherwise each new program stalls the frame that first draws it, 0.3-2 s on an HD 4600 (ANGLE D3D11).
- **Audio voices:** `_hold` disconnects the voice's whole output chain (`head.chain`: filter, panner, reverb send) when its source ends. Disconnecting only the head left every voice ever played wired to its bus, in the audio thread's graph.
- **Lamp cap:** a biome's `lampMax` (blended into `P.lampMax`) caps the *auto* lamps. The bioluminescent bay uses 0.08 because lamps drown the glow. The viewer's 'on' setting overrides the cap.
- **Audio** (`src/audio/`): procedural WebAudio only (no files).
  - The bed is meant to sound like a head under water with plugged ears (the viewer asked for this over accuracy): a brown-noise rumble, a gurgling `slosh` bandpass (130-500 Hz) with surges, sparse low-passed shrimp pops, bubbles in every zone (trails and low glugs, own voice cap) and a night reef `chorus`. All bed buses pass through two lowpasses (1.5 / 1.8 kHz) and a duck that dips under close calls.
  - Do not add broadband noise above ~1 kHz to the bed: it reads as hiss or static. The old pink-noise `wash` / `surge` loops and the dense shrimp crackle (~840 snaps/s) were removed for that reason.
  - Calls come from the nearest 5 callers (3 on low), each with its own timer; ranges and gaps per voice are in `VOICE`. Every voice in `CALLS` is a sound the species is documented to make; list its source in `docs/SOURCES.md` ("Sounds").
  - It reads `creatures.enc` internals (`sp.key`, `dist`, `bounds`, `anchorZ`, `fade`, `done`, `uid`, `n`, `visible`) and `surprises.active.divers`; renaming those silences the calls without throwing.
  - `_build()` closes its AudioContext if it throws; before, every later tap leaked another context.
  - Each zone maps to a family (`ZONE_FAM` -> `FAMILY`) that sets its liquid character: slosh band and pace, the stereo width of the rumble, its own water events (`_water`: lapping, breakers, shifting sand, pebbles, knocks, hollow glugs, distant booms, deep thuds, ice squeaks, vent gushes, gas streams, seeps) and its bubble style. A new zone needs a `ZONE_FAM` entry; without one it falls back to the reef family. A slow mood (spells of 40-90 s), calm nights and storm surge vary the bed over time. The viewer approved this style and asked for variety within it.
  - Animal-linked water sounds: new `e.event` objects (strike, lunge, play, flip, roll), single big bodies passing close (wake), divers and whales near the surface (splash, breach), and the whale-fall crowd (the audio diffs `director.wfCrowd.ev` and plays each event at `wfCrowd.lx/ly/lz`). So the audio also reads `e.event`, `e.n`, `e.bounds.r` and `director.wfCrowd`.
  - Calls carry the caller's identity (`uid * 16 + member`), which fixes its pitch and tempo, its signature whistle, its pod's dialect and its song phrase; species have several call types; group mates answer and big groups sometimes call together. At most one new call starts every 0.35-0.8 s.
  - Pan and rear colouring come from the camera's own matrix (the view the viewer turns) through `_dir()`: a voice behind the view gets a lower lowpass (to 45%) and -3 dB. The rush of water follows `camDirector.craft.v`, and turns or speed changes stir the slosh and send a bubble trail past on the outside of the turn (`_craft`). Ice pings stay at or below 2.7 kHz with a soft onset: as sines up to 4.5 kHz they read as sharp. Long calls (1.5 s and more) are re-aimed while they sound (`_trk`, `_retrack` at 4 Hz). The nearest two groups in `creatures.bg` fill caller slots the near animals leave free, at half the call rate, so the audio also reads those encounters' `dist`, `bounds`, `anchorZ`, `sp`, `fade`, `done`, `uid` and `n`. A tracked call lets go when its animal is done, fading or gone, and a zone jump clears the slots. The reef chorus and the sweepers follow `conditions.clockHours` (full from dusk to about 22:30, a third late at night) and parrotfish do not crunch at night; the audio reads `wfCrowd.ll` (the biter's length) to pitch and pace a tear by shark size.
  - One-shot buffers (`BANK`) are made on first use, then one more variant per tick at most; `bubble()` uses a phasor. Generators run on the main thread: keep them cheap.

## Agent-built subsystems (conventions to keep)
- **Props** (`src/props/`):
  - Layouts run as continuation stages, each ≤ ~5 ms, and merge into batches. LOD is per mesh slice (`userData.lod` plus `THREE.LOD`).
  - Every placement seats on its support through `groundY` / `Ground` in core.js.
  - The new-zone modules (`nb_*.js`, `mangrove.js`, `wall.js`, `softcoral.js`, `deepplain.js`) use `nbFloor` and `seat()` in `nb_common.js`.
  - Registering obstacles, labels with facts, and POIs is mandatory for solid or notable items.
- **Creatures** (`src/creatures/`):
  - `subjects(out)` ids are `uid*1000+i` (animal) or `+999` (group).
  - Night tables blend by `U.uNight`, and POI set pieces have per-type cooldowns.
  - Encounters keep a single hidden class, which matters for V8 speed.
- **Control deck** (`hud.js`, `src/hud/`): talks only to `window.__ocean.app` (main.js). DOM writes are cached and refreshed at ≤ 5 Hz (heading) or 1 Hz slices; sonar and compass are canvases at ≤ 20 fps. No CSS transitions on gauges: restarting them cost 1–2 ms per refresh on the HD 4600.
  - Two levels: the essentials bar (default) and the instrument cockpit (`hud.inst`, localStorage `ocean.deck.mode`; More > Instruments, `G`, or `M` twice). In essentials mode `app.info()` runs at 1 Hz and the sonar and compass canvases neither draw nor resize.
  - Taps travel <= 14 px; beyond that a press on the view is a look drag and never a tap. While the view is turned away, a single tap waits 300 ms for a double tap (recentre). The main canvas has `touch-action: pinch-zoom`. A tap on the Identify card (`#dk-pick .dk-pb`, part of `inUI`) dismisses it at once.
  - Zone picker groups are `ZONE_GROUPS` in `hud/markup.js`. A zone key not listed there falls back by `tempC`, so add new zones to a group.
- **Quality** (`quality.js`):
  - Tiers are low, medium, high and ultra, set by `tierFor()` from the GPU string. The `WQ` macro bakes the tier into every water shader.
  - At runtime, `AdaptiveResolution` steps features first and then resolution, with hysteresis. It uses GPU timer queries when available (`post.gpuMs`).
  - Explicit `?quality=` disables adaptation.
- **Anti-repetition:** `water.js` exports tiling-breakup samplers (`nrTex*`, `nrTri`). Use them instead of a plain `texture2D(uNoiseTex, …)` for large surfaces.
- **Rock pools:** `rock_gen.js` regenerates `T.rocks`, `T.mound`, `T.pinnacle`, `T.bigRocks`, `T.tpRock` and `T.ventRock`, plus vent chimneys, per biome segment. It runs lazily in ≤ 2 ms slices. Always reference these pools live (`tpl: T.rocks`), never as copies (`[...T.rocks]`).
- **Creatures:**
  - Spawn placement (`_placeClear`) pushes every member out of rock and terrain; `_setHome` writes the floor band only at spawn; `_pushOut` slides along sand and walls; open water needs a clear 8-point ring (`_columnClear`); swimmers respect obstacle tops (`OTP`). `devfly.fixTest` reports in-frame corrections that terrainTest cannot see.
  - `sep.js` separates every pair of animals each frame using geometry-derived capsules, sweep-and-prune, and cross-group checks only where boxes overlap.
  - `_terrainFix` checks 8 body extremities against the floor, ceiling and surface.
  - Test tools in devfly.js: `spacingTest`, `terrainTest`, `presenceTest`.
  - The blue whale is scheduled by `director.whaleTick`.
  - **Skin** (`FRAG_SHEEN` in shader.js): silvery flanks carry a narrow mirror term (the surface window and the sun, scaled by sheen squared, flanks only, 0.4x at night); the body's roll and bend drive it, which is the flash that runs through a school. Eyes get a catchlight from the window or the lamps, fin roots are shaded, and fins seen from below pass downwelling light. No texture taps. Jellies take no flank flash and their rims glow faintly under the lamps. Sharks, eels and cetaceans get blotch and crease detail on medium and up only (`#if WQ > 0`, three noise taps). Turtle shells skip the scale netting (it read as cracked mud up close) and get streaked scutes. The sheen's thin-film colour applies only above sheen 0.5 (silvery fish).
  - **Idle life** (creature vertex shader, motion mode 0): hovering fish scull their paired fins and bony fish breathe at the gill cover (both fade with the tail-beat amplitude in `aAnim.y`; the no-gill-cover flag in the low byte of `aCol2.w` leaves out sharks, eels and seals), and dorsal and anal fins ripple all the time. Their rates are whole cycles per `uTime` wrap; the creature vertex shader declares `uTime`.
  - **Smooth turning** (swimmers >= 0.45 m): heading and pitch follow critically damped springs, with the yaw rate capped by `mot.turn[0]` × horizontal speed / L. Swimmers >= 1 m that are not hoverers have almost no turn rate with no way on, so they swim round in arcs instead of pivoting. Their velocity runs along the heading and rotates with it: rocks, startles, separation (which now slows them) and rejoins no longer leave sideways drift. Before this, big animals looked like rigid models rotating: sideslip p95 was 13-64 degrees for sharks, whales and sea lions, now 1-5. `devfly.turnTest` measures it.
  - **Spine bend** (`crBend` / `crArc` in shader.js): `aAnim.z` packs head curvature, tail curvature (both × L) and the dorso-ventral arch at 8 bits each (`packBend`; the octopus keeps its raw jet value). Cross-sections and normals rotate along arcs anchored at 0.4 L, the head leads at the start of a turn and the tail lags. The old bend was a lateral shear of at most 0.06 body lengths that rotated nothing, so the head never looked into the turn.
  - **Per-species turning:** `mot.turn` = [max path curvature × L, spine flex × L, arch × L, bank] in the `--- turning ---` block at the end of species.js; the default per motion mode is `TURN` in encounter.js. Flexible animals (otters, seals, sea lions, eels, most sharks) curl into a deep C; whales, whale sharks, tuna, turtles and rays turn in wide banked arcs. A species made by `merge()` from another before that block needs its own line there.
  - **Big swimmers and the camera** (`camCap`: heading-steered, >= 4 m): they avoid the camera with their body capsule (sep.js), not a sphere of their own size (13.5 m for a 16 m whale, so every close pass slid the whale sideways). They look along the camera's path, aim to keep 6 m + 0.15 L off it and correct mainly by depth, through their heading; as a last resort `_camClear` keeps the lens 1.6 m off the body by sinking or lifting them, never sliding them. Passes come out at 4-10 m. Mantas (under 4 m) are not covered and can still be pushed.
  - **Blocked big swimmers:** against rock or ice they may turn slowly on the spot and only creep forward (polar orcas used to wedge and crab).
  - **Otters** duck-dive (roll while swimming, head under first), and the head leads pitch changes (`leadV`), which arches the back (`BV`).
  - `reset()` (zone jump) empties both creatures and surprises; `pickRay(o, d, max)` (global origin) serves tap-to-identify; `subjects()` entries carry the species `key`.
  - Shoals that hug an ice ceiling are expensive: Arctic cod pressed to the floes cost 216 ms per frame; a loose shoal 1-2.6 m below them costs 0.75 ms.

Khmer/pagoda crowns (`prangTop`) follow the same rule. Before this, decayed buildings left domes floating. Audit: `node tools/audit/domes.mjs`.

Its `fillItems()` mirrors `Batch.build` in core.js: change them together. Whole-batch merges of ~11k vertices cost 6-12 ms per step before this.

- **Lost City** (`nb5_lostcity.js`, `newbiomes5.js`): a terraced serpentinite massif with carbonate chimneys built by `columnG` row lathes, scaled up for the big towers.
  - The landmark per segment comes from `biomes.lostCityTowerOf(g)`: a bench in `floor`, placed on the outside of a bend.

- **Cold-water coral** (`nb6_*.js`, `newbiomes6.js`): `coldCoral(g)` in biomes.js lists each segment's mounds, elongated along the current; about a third of segments are Darwin-Mounds style; one trawl scar and one summit POI per segment.

  - Reef fabric is `layoutCcC`: a dark framework tangle on a jittered 0.64 m lattice (the step divides 64 m, so chunk borders match) plus instanced bush fields (`nb6_lophelia.bush`, 120 triangles; 520 / 950 / 1600 per chunk for low / medium / high). The detailed `colonyJob` colonies are a hero layer within about 11 m of the path. A painted shell alone read as a flat pale sheet.
  - `floorTint(x, s, nn, T, g)` may set `T[6]` (ripple multiplier) and `T[7]` (added rockiness); other zones get 1 and 0.
- **Whale Fall** (`nb7_whalefall.js`, `newbiomes7.js`, `creatures/wf_crowd.js`): a short (340-420 m), slow zone around one whale carcass per segment (`biomes.whaleFallOf(g)`). A lamp-lit deep variant (1,675 m, sleeper sharks, hagfish, an old-skeleton stage) was built first and removed on 2026-11-15 at the viewer's request.
  - Sunlit, as the viewer asked (daytime and a feeding crowd): Hawaii, 25 °C, about 14 m down on pale sand. Tiger sharks and oceanic whitetips feed together (6 / 9 / 12 by tier: 12 at once is the documented maximum), with mahi-mahi, small fish, crabs and rarely a very large white shark.
  - Placement: the outside of a bend, mid-segment, so the cross-fades into the neighbours start 50+ m beyond the carcass; on the segment the camera starts in, `lenAhead` stretches that segment (`BiomeSequence._ahead`). `cruise` is a per-zone speed factor, blended into `P.cruise` in main.js.
  - `carcassPlan(seed)` makes every carcass different: pose, girth, eaten amount E (0-1), wounds (the tail stock always opened to bone), flippers and flukes. The carcass is a composite (a ~17-23 m rorqual; the documented Hawaiian carcasses were sperm whales, and the well-documented feeding events are at floating or reef-grounded carcasses), so no label states species, length or depth, and facts are worded about the documented events.
  - The carcass is drawn as its own mesh over the template arrays: merging it into a batch cost ~9 ms in one stage. Pale material gets a lamp-proportional glow that grows with distance (zero by day), as at Lost City. It uses `PAL_SUN` (graded for the water, as the reef palettes are) and lies on a lava flat (`biomes.wfLavaAt`, the `wfLava` rock provider), sparse within ~15 m of it.
  - The crowd (`wf_crowd.js`): the sharks swim with their own steering (loops round the body, run-ins, sweeps, arrivals, departures), but the bite is posed through `Encounter.HOLD` (`_holdPose` at the top of `_writeAll`; a held member is skipped by separation; posed turns are capped at 2.2 rad/s): since big swimmers keep way on and the camera bubble pushes them off the near flank, their own steering left the mouth 4-6 m from the flesh. Feeding spots all around the body are `POI data.ring`; a bigger shark displaces a smaller one; a torn-off chunk makes a burst of `FXK.SCRAP` particles and the fish rush in. Events in view call `pace.mark()` and pull the camera's gaze through `gx` / `gy` / `gs` / `gw` in the POI data.
  - The fish clouds are `bait` groups whose centre is moved like an orbit. `wf_crowd._school` re-clamps their slots, because `_slotShift` in encounter.js lets separation grow a bait ball's slots without limit (rings of 100-500 m after one pass); other bait balls that sharks pass through probably have the same problem.
  - The crowd's guard (`watch(p, WF_CAST)`, `_guard`) fades out animals near the carcass that are not in the zone's cast.
  - The camera holds the `whalefall` POI for the whole pass (`_wfHold` in camera.js, from 55 m before the tail), sweeps its gaze along the body 3 m ahead of its own place, and eases back to the route after the head.
- **Far field** (`props/farfield.js`, registered last by `registerFarField` in main.js): a cheap world-space layer of low-poly props per zone family, from the edge of the flight tube out to the zone's visibility. The hero populators only dress about 15-40 m beside the route on a seabed 165 m wide each side, so the world read as a bright ribbon through bare ground (the viewer: "completely desolate" to the sides and back).
  - Placement: a global jittered grid per zone (`FAR[key].cell`), gated by `biomes.habitat(x, s)` (gardens, clusters and bare stretches in world space, not along the route; kelp uses `kelpGrove`, seagrass `seagrassMeadow`). `habitat` also scales the floor's encrusting-life tint (terrain_gen.js) and the reef's own framework and coral heads (`rich()` in reef.js): change it and you change all three.
  - Hero footprints: `registerFarField` wraps every populator registered before it and stamps their meshes into a 1 m top-height grid per chunk in budgeted steps, so far items keep off hero props (many populators register no footprints). A populator registered after the far field is not seen. Items within 16 m of the route go through `core.propClear` (flight tube).
  - Budget: one merged batch per material per chunk; a per-chunk triangle cap (`CAP` per tier x `capK` per zone; low 9k), cells tried nearest band first with 30% kept for the far kinds beyond 45 m; coarse twins (`tpl.lo`) beyond 20 m on low, 55 m elsewhere; on low, swaying kinds only where `swayLow` is set. On low the layer is 1-9% of the forward frame's triangles and about +5 draw calls. Far templates are `solid = false` with no labels; only items taller than `big` and wreck hull outlines register an obstacle (at most 16 per chunk).

The same tuning for kelp, blue hole, mangrove, vents and cold coral was tried and dropped: the side views were not clearly better.

- **Background life** (`creatures/background.js`): animals beside and behind the route, so the water is inhabited in every direction (the near system spawns ahead in a band along the route and retired groups once they were behind).
  - Residents: groups from the zone's own table on the route's flanks (per-zone `BG`; from 8-12 m off the route out to where the species still reads, `reachOf`: ~28 body lengths, ~45 for schools, at most 35% of the fog distance), placed mostly ahead so travel carries them past the sides and behind; patches from a noise field; reef fish on the highest nearby structure. No rare highlights, whales, or resting / burrowing / walking kinds; lamp-lit zones only get animals that glow; caves, tide pools, hadal, Lost City and cold-water coral get none.
  - Passed groups: a group the near system would retire behind or beside the camera carries on in the background (`Encounter.passed`) until it is too far to read or its slot is needed. Retirement is by distance, age and budget, never just for being behind.

They do not steer round rock: adopt or spawn only clear of it, and `_bgRock` re-checks (fade or drop).

Counts per tier are `TIER` (20 groups on low).

- **Spawns belong to their zone:** `spawnOne` probes the zone weights up to the fog distance ahead, so a neighbour's animals used to appear 100+ m short of its boundary (golden jellies around a whale fall). A spawn is now refused where its table's zone weighs under 0.4 at the spawn position, and retried once with the tables of that position.
- **Reef look** (`reef.js`, `reef_shapes.js`): palettes are written as the corals look in air, then graded for the water by `RL()` (+35 % red, +15 % saturation), because red dies about four times faster than blue over the light path at 9 m. Do not grade them again elsewhere.

- **Pacing** (`creatures/pacing.js`): tracks time since the last highlight in view. When one is overdue (100 s, or 150 s in the sparse deep zones) it boosts the zone's own highlight-class candidates and shortens the delight cooldowns. It never adds animals, and it can do little in Jellyfish Lake, Tide Pools and the Hadal Trench, whose casts have no highlight-class species: those stay sparse on purpose.
  - Silverside and anchovy schools of >= 40 can be struck in zones with a `HUNTERS` entry, at most every 150 s (only `plan.sleepy` schools are spared at night). Hunters lunge along their heading (the `free` branch in `_steer`), and milling species strike as schools: before, strike sideslip was about 140 degrees at the 95th percentile.
  - The watchability harness (moments, highlights, lulls, kinds and species share per zone) is described in the 2026-11-14 changelog entry; the measure that separates zones is highlights and species share, not the raw moment rate.

## Debugging
- `__ocean.advance(seconds)` steps the simulation without rAF (the preview pane throttles rAF). `__ocean.bench(w, h, n)` measures GPU ms per frame at a fixed size. Benchmarks are unreliable while other tabs render the app.
- `window.__ocean` exposes the scene, camera, terrain, shape, seq, creatures, post and U.
- `window.__props` holds per-populator timings and triangle counts.
- `?cr=<species>&crstats=1` forces a creature species and logs creature stats.
- `tools/shot.cjs` captures the app headless (software GL): it gates rAF after boot, skips drawing while it steps the sim, then renders a few real frames. Read the canvas in the same task as the last tick. Useful for before/after checks without a GPU; timings from it are not GPU timings.
